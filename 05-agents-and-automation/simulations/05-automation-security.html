Defending an agent: the trifecta, five layers, one boundary

Where the lethal access is, and how to take it away

The lethal trifecta

An agent becomes dangerous when it can do all three of these at once:

  1. Read private data (alerts, logs, who owns what)
  2. See untrusted content (a ticket comment an outsider wrote)
  3. Talk to the outside world (send email, call a web hook)

With all three, a poisoned comment can tell the agent to gather secrets and mail them out - exfiltration. Remove any one leg and that attack falls apart. Toggle the legs and watch the risk:

Analogy - a clerk with keys, an open mailbox, and a stranger's note. A clerk who can open the filing cabinet, read a note slipped under the door, and post letters can be tricked into mailing your files to a stranger. Take away the stamps (no outside mail) and the same trick achieves nothing.
SOC: Lab 5 breaks the trifecta by giving the triage agent no external tool at all - it can read and take notes, but it cannot email anyone. The leak leg is simply gone.

Downgrading the risk - the checklist

Least privilege (give only the tools the job needs) · Human approval for anything that changes production or leaves the building · Validate arguments even on approved actions · Treat every tool result as data, never as orders (wrap it in <observation>) · Limits + an audit log so nothing runs away and everything is recorded. Same spirit as Modules 1-4: assume the input is hostile, and put the control in code, not in the prompt.
