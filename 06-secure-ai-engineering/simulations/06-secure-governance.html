Data governance

Governance is simply knowing, for every piece of data the system touches: how sensitive is it, does it contain personal data, and where is it allowed to live? You cannot protect or justify what you have not classified.

Analogy - labels in a hospital. A hospital does not treat a lunch menu and a patient file the same way. Each document is labelled, and the label decides who may see it and where it may go. Data classification is those labels for your system.
SOC / DevOps: auth logs and CMDB data are confidential and contain PII; ticket comments are untrusted input. The classification is what later tells you this system must be hosted locally (Section 4).

The three governance questions

Sensitivity (public / internal / confidential / regulated) decides the controls. PII? decides privacy obligations. Residency (where it may live) decides the deployment pattern. In this system every asset is on-VM - nothing is sent to any outside service, which is the whole point of local hosting.
