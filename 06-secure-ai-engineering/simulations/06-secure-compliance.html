One control, many frameworks - each backed by a test

Compliance mapping

Compliance mapping turns your engineering controls into language an auditor or CISO signs off: for each control, which framework clause does it help satisfy, and what is the evidence? Do it once and the same control answers many frameworks at once.

Analogy - one ingredient, many recipes. Eggs appear in the omelette, the cake and the custard. One control (say least privilege) appears in NIST, ISO and SOC 2. You map it once and reuse it everywhere.
In this system: the ten Lab 5/6 controls map across NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, ISO 27001 and SOC 2 - and each clause links back to a passing test as evidence.

One control, four frameworks

Pick a control to see the clauses it satisfies - straight from controls.py . Notice how one engineering control covers several frameworks at once.

Evidence is what makes it defendable

A mapping with no evidence is a wish-list. Here, every control names a Lab 5 test; running link_lab5.py executes them so each clause is backed by a passing test, not a promise. That is what turns "we think it's secure" into "here is the proof" in front of a CISO.
